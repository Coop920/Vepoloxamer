Why severe oral mucositis is the lead program

The choice of lead indication was driven by three criteria — biology, market, trial-design feasibility, and competitive position.

1. Direct Biological Fit

Radiation-induced mucositis is driven by membrane damage to oral epithelium — precisely what the mechanism addresses. Having Vepoloxamer circulating prior to dosing positions the drug to act the moment radiation damage begins.

2. Zero Approved Drugs

Approximately 90,000 new head and neck cancer patients receive radiation each year across the US, EU and UK. 60–70% develop severe Grade 3+ mucositis. There is no FDA-approved drug to prevent it. >$1B total addressable market.

3. Clean Trial Design

Scheduled radiation creates a defined dosing window and a homogeneous cohort. WHO and CTCAE grading provide quantifiable, FDA-recognized endpoints. A focused Phase 1b/2 produces a fast, clear readout.

Pre-clinical work in acute trauma and burns; the "Time Is Tissue" indication

  • A severe burn keeps growing after the injury. The zone of stasis — injured but still-perfused tissue at the wound margin — progresses to necrosis over 24 to 72 hours, and no approved drug intervenes in that window.
  • Vepoloxamer partitions selectively into damaged cell membranes and not intact ones, making it mechanistically matched to exactly this problem. A 2024 PRISMA-compliant systematic review of 33 preclinical studies concluded that P188 is beneficial across thermal and electrical burn injury models and warrants clinical investigation.1
  • Vepoloxamer is being supplied under a material transfer agreement to an academic burn research program for preclinical evaluation.
  • Burns is our proof of concept for the platform: a lesion that is anatomically bounded, externally visible, and reads out in days rather than months. Establish that early membrane stabilization preserves tissue there, and the same clock governs stroke, traumatic brain injury, hemorrhagic shock, and limb ischemia.

Lead Program

Nearly 90,000 patients a year face an untreatable toxicity.

Severe oral mucositis is the most consequential acute toxicity of head and neck cancer radiation therapy — and there is no FDA-approved drug to prevent it. Our lead program is built to change that.

What severe oral mucositis does

Oral mucositis is the breakdown of the oral mucosa under the cumulative effect of fractionated radiation. In its severe (Grade 3 or 4) form it produces ulceration across the mouth and throat that makes eating, swallowing and speaking difficult or impossible — during the most intensive phase of cancer treatment.

Why Vepoloxamer fits

Radiation generates membrane-damaging reactive oxygen species in epithelial cells; the cells lose integrity; an inflammatory cascade follows. Vepoloxamer, administered before a radiation fraction, is kinetically positioned to act at the moment membrane damage begins.

🎯

Mechanistically Aligned

Direct DNA damage accounts for only about a third of the injury to basal epithelial cells in mucositis. The rest is downstream — reactive oxygen species, lipid peroxidation of the cell membrane, and breakdown of the epithelial barrier. Vepoloxamer reseals those damaged membranes. It does not shield cells from radiation itself, and it does not interfere with the DNA-level effects that drive tumor kill.

⏱️

Scheduled Insult, Scheduled Drug

Unlike acute indications where the timing of injury is unpredictable, radiation is delivered on a defined fractionated schedule. The prophylactic dosing window is physician-controlled. This is the right paradigm for the molecule — and it directly eliminates the enrollment heterogeneity that undermined prior trials.

📊

Preclinical Validation

In internal preclinical research, Vepoloxamer given before focal lung irradiation confined radiation damage to the targeted area while leaving tumor control unaffected — the profile of a clinically useful radioprotectant: healthy tissue shielded, tumor not.

The development plan

A focused Phase 1b/2 adaptive trial in head and neck cancer patients receiving curative-intent radiation, leveraging an existing regulatory package, established drug substance, and AI-driven approaches to protocol design and execution.
