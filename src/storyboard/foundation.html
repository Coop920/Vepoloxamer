Two Phase 3 trials. Neither met its primary endpoint. Here is what happened.

CytRx, 1998–1999 — sickle cell VOC

A Phase 3 trial of purified poloxamer 188 in sickle cell vaso-occlusive crisis (n=255 of 350 planned; halted early for capital constraints, not safety). The trial did not meet its primary endpoint on the full population, but showed a mean 8.8-hour reduction in crisis duration (p=0.04), with larger effects in children ≤15 years and in hydroxyurea users.

→ Orringer et al., JAMA 2001

Mast Therapeutics, 2013–2016 — EPIC Phase 3

EPIC (n=388) evaluated Vepoloxamer in sickle cell vaso-occlusive crisis. It did not meet its primary endpoint: the point estimate favored Vepoloxamer over placebo in shortening crisis duration, but the difference was small and not statistically significant, against the high patient-to-patient variability of sickle cell pain. Adverse events were broadly comparable to placebo, with no drug-related deaths. Full results were published in JAMA (Casella et al., 2021).

→ Casella et al., JAMA 2021

The same molecule, in the right paradigm.

Prior sponsors advanced Vepoloxamer into acute-rescue indications that are hard for any therapeutic: heterogeneous patient populations, unpredictable timing of presentation, and endpoints that struggle to detect a modest effect. Sickle cell crisis is the hardest of them — patients present at different points in the injury cascade, and pain is confounded by opioid use.

Additionally, the indication was based on exploiting the rheological properties of Vepoloxamer, not membrane sealing. LifeRaft is taking the drug into a new indication that fits with the membrane-sealing mechanism of action.

The path to LifeRaft

Vepoloxamer's development has advanced through several sponsors over more than three decades. The chain of custody is documented and the asset has been continuously stewarded.

1980s–1990s

Origin and the purification breakthrough — CytRx Corporation

Dr. Robert Hunter at Emory characterized poloxamer 188's cytoprotective properties. CytRx licensed the work, developed the supercritical fluid chromatography process that removes the low-molecular-weight impurities responsible for renal toxicity in commercial P188, and secured the composition-of-matter patent that remains in force today.

1996–1999

The RheothRx era — and the lesson that defined the molecule

Trials of the unpurified material in acute myocardial infarction produced a renal signal. That signal was later traced to the low-molecular-weight impurity fraction — the finding that made purification the central fact of this asset. A CytRx-sponsored Phase 3 in sickle cell followed with the purified material, exploiting the rheological properties of Vepoloxamer.

2004–2011

Asset consolidation — SynthRx and Adventrx

SynthRx acquired all rights from CytRx in 2004. Adventrx Pharmaceuticals acquired SynthRx in 2010 and subsequently renamed itself Mast Therapeutics. Composition-of-matter prosecution advanced through this period.

2011–2016

Mast Therapeutics — the EPIC Phase 3

Mast reestablished GMP manufacturing, earned FDA Fast Track and Orphan Drug designations, and ran EPIC across 66 hospitals in 12 countries. The trial missed its endpoint and generated a clean safety profile.

2017–Present

LifeRaft Biosciences

LifeRaft acquired the composition-of-matter patent, the regulatory package, the prior datasets and approximately 140 kg of GMP drug substance. A research collaboration with the Medical University of South Carolina generated new preclinical data supporting use in radiation-induced injury — the foundation for the current lead program. Operating leadership and a scientific advisory board were appointed in 2026 to advance the molecule into clinical proof of concept in indications that benefit most from the membrane-sealing mechanism of action.

A layered approach to commercial exclusivity

Exclusivity is built in layers — a granted composition-of-matter patent at the foundation, method-of-use protection through granted and pending filings, an active continuation strategy, and regulatory exclusivity that stacks independently of any patent outcome. The structure is designed so that protection does not depend on a single filing.

Layer 1 · Granted

Composition of Matter

Covers the purified Vepoloxamer molecule itself and the supercritical fluid chromatography purification process — the most foundational and defensible form of pharmaceutical IP. Granted in the United States, Europe, Japan, Korea, Australia and Taiwan.

Floor: 2035
Layer 2 · In Prosecution

Method of Use

Broad claims to administering Vepoloxamer to protect non-targeted cells from radiation damage and other forms of cellular injury. Granted in Japan. Under prosecution in the United States and Europe, with a parallel continuation strategy in place.

Target: 2041+
Layer 3 · Active

Continuation Strategy

An active continuation program layering filings around specific formulations, dosing regimens, sub-populations and new indications — including new method-of-use filings generated through academic sponsored research, each carrying an independent term from filing.
