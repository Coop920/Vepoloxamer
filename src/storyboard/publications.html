The pathobiology of mucositis ★
Sonis · Nature Reviews Cancer · 2004
Establishes the five-phase model of mucositis — initiation, primary damage response, signal amplification, ulceration, healing — reframing OM from epithelial injury to an ROS-initiated, cytokine-amplified submucosal process.
The most-cited foundational paper in the field. Authored by LifeRaft's SAB Chair.

Avasopasem manganese treatment for severe oral mucositis from chemoradiotherapy for locally advanced head and neck cancer: phase 3 randomized controlled trial (ROMAN) ★
Anderson et al. · eClinicalMedicine · 2025 · PMID 41127563
455 randomized / 407 analyzed: SOM incidence 54% vs 64% (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.71–1.00, p=0.045), with reduced SOM duration and delayed onset. Received an FDA Complete Response Letter.
The competitive benchmark. Note the borderline p-value and CI touching 1.00 — this is the bar, and it is a low one.

The broadening scope of oral mucositis and oral ulcerative mucosal toxicities of anticancer therapies ★
Elad, Yarom, Zadik, Kuten-Shorrer & Sonis · CA: A Cancer Journal for Clinicians · 2022
60–70% of head & neck chemoradiation patients develop WHO Grade 3–4 mucositis, initiating in the 15–30 Gy window. Only about one-third of the injury is direct DNA damage; the remainder is a ROS-driven cascade through NF-κB, DAMPs/TLR-4 and the ceramide pathway, ending in increased membrane permeability and breakdown of the epithelial cells.
The canonical modern review — and the paper that names membrane failure as a step in the field's five-stage model.

Direct observation of poloxamer 188 insertion into lipid monolayers ★
Maskarinec et al. · Biophysical Journal · 2002
P188 inserts into lipid monolayers only below a threshold surface pressure and is squeezed out as the monolayer recovers — the biophysical basis for selective targeting of damaged membranes and for self-limiting action.
THE critical mechanism paper. It is why prophylactic dosing is kinetic positioning, not insertion into healthy membranes.
