The Science

Vepoloxamer: a purified, pharmaceutical-grade poloxamer therapeutic

P188 is a non-ionic triblock copolymer — two hydrophilic polyethylene oxide (PEO) arms flanking a hydrophobic polypropylene oxide (PPO) core. P188 is the unpurified form of the drug, and has been used as a surfactant and excipient. Vepoloxamer is LifeRaft's patented purified form of P188 in which the low molecular weight fractions linked to renal toxicity seen with commercial-grade P188 have been removed.

Unlike most therapeutics, which target a single receptor or enzyme, Vepoloxamer operates at a more fundamental level: the cell membrane itself. It integrates into damaged membranes, speeding cell repair and limiting the cascade effect of reactive oxygenated species damaging yet more tissue.

Characterized Mechanisms of Action

What was historically described as a single mechanism — membrane resealing — is now understood as a coordinated set of activities. Each has been characterized in peer-reviewed publications across multiple laboratories.

🧬

Selective Membrane Sealing

The hydrophobic core inserts into membranes only where lipid packing has been disrupted — below a threshold surface pressure — and is squeezed back out as the membrane recovers. This is why the molecule targets damaged cells and why its action is self-limiting.

⚡

Protection Against Lipid Peroxidation

Sealed membranes physically interrupt the Reactive Oxygen Species-driven secondary injury cycle that destroys tissue downstream of the initial insult. Polymer hydrophilicity governs the ability to protect membranes from peroxidation. Tissue and cellular damage is often a cascade, with the initial damage to tissue leading to further downstream damage. Vepoloxamer reduces that subsequent cascading damage that is driven by Reactive Oxygen Species.

🔥

Anti-Inflammatory Signaling

Sealed membranes prevent spillage of damage-associated molecular patterns that drive inflammation, reducing NF-κB activation and downstream cytokine release across contexts from lung injury to cardiac ischemia.

💨

Endothelial Nitric Oxide Signaling

Recent work in Scientific Reports shows that cardioprotection is mediated through increased endothelial nitric oxide production — a mechanism distinct from membrane sealing. Inhibiting nitric oxide synthase abolishes the protective effect entirely. Characterized in cardiac tissue specifically.

📚

The Science Is Still Being Written

Vepoloxamer's mechanisms continue to be characterized by independent academic groups. The 2025 nitric-oxide and intracellular-transport findings both expanded what is known about how the molecule works.

Two Clinical Windows: Prior to Scheduled Tissue Damage, and After Tissue Damage

A defining feature of the biology is timing flexibility. The molecule can be deployed before a scheduled treatment where damage to non-targeted tissue is a concern, ensuring Vepoloxamer is circulating prior to the insult — or immediately after an acute insult. In both cases the goal is the same: to limit the cascade of secondary damage.

The right indication.

Prior development programs advanced this molecule into indications with heterogeneous patient populations and challenging endpoints where the mechanism of action did not provide the most patient benefit. LifeRaft is the first company to advance Vepoloxamer in an indication where the insult to cells is scheduled — the paradigm that aligns most directly with the mechanism, and that creates a homogeneous cohort, a defined dosing window, and an unambiguous clinical readout.
